In first interview as FDA oncology chief, Angelo de Claro vows to ‘continue the innovation’ behind OCE legacy

In his first interview after taking the reins of the FDA’s Oncology Center of Excellence, Angelo de Claro, M.D., is outlining a regulatory agenda that prioritizes organizational strength and continued regulatory modernization.

Stepping into one of the most heavyweight leadership roles in U.S. drug evaluation, de Claro faces the tall order of succeeding an FDA legend. In his first interview, the new oncology chief vowed to build on his predecessor’s foundation while pioneering frameworks to integrate artificial intelligence into regulatory reviews, streamline drug development, and boost U.S. trial participation.

De Claro rose to director of the OCE at a time when the FDA underwent another near-complete revamp of senior leadership. Initially tapped as acting OCE director in late 2025, he became in May the first senior FDA leader on the drug evaluation side to be confirmed in a permanent capacity during this tumultuous period.

His background as an FDA insider brings a crucial sense of regulatory stability and institutional continuity to the agency — and to an oncology master office that oversees some of the most high-stakes approvals in modern medicine. In 2025, the OCE’s work led to 17 approvals of new oncology molecules and 38 new indications for existing drugs, according to the division’s annual report published in June.

His appointment followed the departure of the iconic founding director of the OCE, who altogether led the FDA’s oncology division for more than two decades. Under that tenure, the FDA created transformative review pathways and pioneered various regulatory initiatives that continue to guide the efficient and safe development of cancer drugs.

In a written interview, de Claro expressed a sense of continuity and respect for what his predecessor built over the past two decades, saying the groundwork laid there is being used to continue to effectively execute the mission and vision of the OCE.

He listed his priorities as retaining and hiring additional staff, deploying AI tools to aid in review, streamlining oncology drug development from both regulatory and clinical trial standpoints, allowing for more U.S. patients to be enrolled in clinical trials, and continuing external oncology community engagement.

That strive for efficiency was recently on display during the FDA’s review of a breakthrough pancreatic cancer drug. In a compressed timeline, the agency wrapped up its evaluation and granted approval more than six months ahead of the target PDUFA date, as de Claro noted in a press release last month.

The drug’s unprecedented efficacy in a notoriously difficult-to-treat cancer drew the attention of the FDA’s review team, who de Claro said recognized the importance of the application and were dedicated to completing the work. The OCE used the real-time oncology review framework, which enables an earlier start to evaluation before the sponsor completes its application.

Angelo de Claro, M.D., Director, FDA Oncology Center of Excellence (FDA)

De Claro said that in general, when topline results are received during early clinical trials, the OCE may proactively start working with a company to ensure that their application meets review needs — stressing the importance of upfront communication in moving an application faster.

The expedited review was supported in part by an accelerated review mechanism reserved for therapies that address certain U.S. health priorities. The pilot has drawn controversy, including fears that shifting significant resources to fast-track high-profile applications could cause delays for other standard reviews. Addressing that concern, de Claro said those reviews will not come at the expense of other review deadlines.
