Gov. Abbott announces working group to develop framework for 3-year bachelor’s degrees

Gov. Greg Abbott forms a team to devise three-year bachelor’s degrees in Texas, aiming to cut college costs by up to 25% and boost career readiness.

AUSTIN, Texas — Gov. Greg Abbott on Wednesday named 13 members to serve on the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board’s working group to develop a framework for establishing three-year bachelor’s degree pathways.

Abbott said Texas students deserve a clear and affordable pathway to high-paying jobs, and that the working group will deliver a statewide framework for three-year bachelor’s degrees to keep the state a national leader in affordable, accessible, high-quality education.

Those chosen for the working group include representatives from the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board, the Lone Star College System, the Texas A&M University System, the Texas State University System, Texas Southern University, the University of Houston, Texas Tech University, the University of North Texas, Tyler Junior College, South Texas College, Grayson College, Texas Woman’s University and the University of Texas System.

Abbott said a three-year bachelor’s degree could reduce the cost of earning a degree by up to 25 percent, saving Texas students between $15,000 to $35,000.

Texas Higher Education Commissioner Wynn Rosser said he looked forward to hearing from the expert educators as they identify accelerated programs that can offer students a quality education leading to a durable, financially rewarding career.

The group has been asked to develop recommendations about academic standards and requirements for the pathways; the degree programs and high-demand occupations for which they may be most appropriate; student demand and employer workforce needs; accreditation and institutional approval requirements; and any statutory, regulatory or administrative changes necessary for statewide implementation.

This group will submit recommendations and a proposed framework to the Office of the Governor by Dec. 31, 2026.
